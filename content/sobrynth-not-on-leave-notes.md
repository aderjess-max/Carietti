## What changed and why

1. **Added the section the draft was circling but never wrote: "How do you measure what didn't
   happen?"** This is the piece's real subject. The best outcome Sobrynth produces leaves no
   trace — no claim, no leave, no line item — and that is precisely why prevention gets
   under-funded. Naming the tension out loud, and conceding it before a skeptical benefits leader
   can, is what turns a nice anecdote into an argument. "The costs are loud and countable. The
   saves are silent."
2. **Put the reader's own workforce in the story.** 1 in 10 employees has an untreated substance
   use disorder; 70% of adults with one hold jobs. "These aren't people on leave. They're in the
   meeting. They're hitting their numbers." Without that, the story is about a stranger.
3. **Cut the three-beat repetition** ("Moments when… Moments when… Moments when"). It was the one
   place the writing got incantatory instead of concrete.
4. **"Why the phone call is the product."** The draft explained the peer coaches; it never said
   why immediacy is the design decision. Added: the moment that matters rarely arrives during
   business hours or with a diagnosis attached.
5. **Tightened the confidentiality beat** into the deck's own language — no claim filed, no
   diagnosis shared, no data back to the employer — and named why it exists: "Removing the fear is
   the entire reason the benefit gets used."
6. **Kept "A difficult afternoon. Nothing more."** on its own line. It's the best sentence in the
   draft and it was buried mid-paragraph.
7. **New closing line** so the piece lands rather than trails: "Sometimes the most valuable thing
   a benefit does is make sure nothing happens at all."
8. Six headers, and a CTA the original didn't have.

## Title

Retitled from "The Crisis That Never Happened" (2026-08-25). The original was good, but the
previous article is "Before Crisis Becomes a Claim" — two consecutive pieces built on "crisis"
read as a sequel rather than a second idea. The new title is a line lifted from the article, and
it does something the old one couldn't: it corrects a belief the reader is holding right now.
Benefits leaders picture substance use as an absence event; this says it is sitting in the room.

The old title survives as the eyebrow on cover A, so the phrase isn't lost.
