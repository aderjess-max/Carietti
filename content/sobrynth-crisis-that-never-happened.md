# The Crisis That Never Happened

It was just after 2:00 p.m.

An employee was staring down a deadline. Emails were stacking up. Their anxiety was climbing by the minute.

Five years earlier, they had stopped drinking.

That afternoon, all they could think about was having one.

## Nobody in the building knew

Their manager didn't notice. Their coworkers couldn't tell. To everyone in the room, it looked like an ordinary workday.

Instead of leaving, shutting down, or trying to white-knuckle it alone, they picked up their phone and called Sobrynth.

Within minutes they were talking with a peer coach — someone who has sat in that exact chair, who knows what five years of recovery feels like on a bad afternoon, and who knew what to do with it.

No judgment. No paperwork. No intake form. No worry that their employer would find out, because their employer never does.

They talked it through. Then they went back to work.

The deadline was met. The work got done. Their coworkers never knew a crisis had been unfolding a few feet away.

## The moments that never become data

Employers think about substance use in the vocabulary of outcomes: treatment episodes, leaves of absence, healthcare claims, workplace incidents.

But every one of those outcomes began as a moment like this one — a bad afternoon, a decision made alone, sometimes years before anything reached a report.

Roughly 1 in 10 employees is living with an untreated substance use disorder right now, and 70% of adults with a substance use disorder hold jobs. These aren't people on leave. They're in the meeting. They're hitting their numbers. And on the hardest days, most of them have no one to call.

Without support, an afternoon like that can become missed work, a slide in performance, a damaged relationship — or a return to use.

With support, it stays what it should have been.

A difficult afternoon. Nothing more.

## Why the phone call is the product

Sobrynth was built around immediate, confidential peer support, because the moment that matters rarely arrives during business hours or with a diagnosis attached.

Every one of our peer coaches has lived experience with substance use and recovery. They're certified and clinically supervised, and they understand the moments that never make it into a medical record, because they've had them.

Employees reach out anonymously and get support in real time. No claim is filed. No diagnosis is shared. No data goes back to the employer. Removing the fear is the entire reason the benefit gets used.

## How do you measure what didn't happen?

Here's the honest tension in all of this: the best outcome we produce is invisible.

There's no claim to point at. No leave to compare against a baseline. No line item labeled "relapse avoided." The most valuable thing that happened in that office at 2 p.m. was nothing.

Which is exactly why prevention is so easy to under-invest in. The costs of substance use are loud and countable — the $8,817 per affected employee per year that the National Safety Council and NORC put on it, the turnover, the six-figure claims. The saves are silent.

But they're real, and they are happening in your workforce in both directions right now. Some of your people have somewhere to turn on a hard afternoon. Most don't.

## What we're actually counting

The deadline that wasn't missed.

The conversation no one else ever knew took place.

Those are the moments that build steadier people, stronger teams, and a workplace where someone doesn't have to face the worst hour of their week alone.

Sometimes the most valuable thing a benefit does is make sure nothing happens at all.
