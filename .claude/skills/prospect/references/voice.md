# Voice

The messages have to sound like a 23-year operator who has carried a $12M number, not like a
consultant with a funnel. Everything below is in service of that.

## The shape of a good message

1. **An observation about them.** True, specific, verifiable. Two lines maximum.
2. **The insight.** What that observation usually means — the failure mode they're walking
   into. This is the part a generalist can't write.
3. **The credential, briefly.** Years, quota, vertical. One line. It is proof, not the pitch.
4. **A small ask.** Twenty minutes. With an easy way to decline.

Total: under 150 words. Founders skim on phones.

## Rules

- **Write to one person.** Never "you guys," never "your team" when you mean the founder.
- **Lead with them.** The first sentence is about their company. Never about you.
- **One specific detail beats three general claims.** "Your AE req has been up since June"
  outperforms "I noticed you're growing."
- **Short sentences.** Break long ones. Operators write in fragments and it reads as confidence.
- **Name the failure, not the benefit.** "Your reps will stall in security review" lands.
  "I help you build a scalable sales process" does not.
- **Give them an out.** "If the timing's off I'll get out of your way." Counterintuitively
  raises reply rate — it signals you have other options.
- **No em-dash pileups, no rhetorical questions stacked, no triads.** One rhetorical
  question per message at most.
- **Never claim a result you can't source.** No invented percentages, no "I helped a company
  like yours 3x their pipeline" unless it's true and you'd name it on a call.

## The kill list

Do not send anything containing these. Rewrite instead.

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| "I hope this email finds you well" | Signals mass send |
| "I wanted to reach out" / "just reaching out" | Filler; delete the sentence |
| "quick question" as a subject | Burned by a decade of SDR use |
| "circling back" / "bumping this" / "following up" | Says nothing new is coming |
| "synergy," "leverage" (as a verb), "solutions," "space" | Consultant-speak |
| "game-changer," "unlock," "supercharge," "10x" | Marketing register |
| "as you may know" / "as I'm sure you're aware" | Condescending |
| "Let me know if you'd like to learn more" | Passive close, no ask |
| "at your earliest convenience" | Formal register that doesn't match the rest |
| Any stat without a source | Erodes the credential |
| Exclamation points | One is too many here |
| "worth a conversation" / "worth a chat" / "worth exploring" / any "worth a ___" | Reads as AI-generated on sight. Her call, 2026-08-18. Ask plainly instead: "Any chance you have 20 minutes?" |

## Subject lines

Lowercase or sentence case. Three to five words. Should read like an internal email from a
colleague, not a campaign.

Good: `your AE search` · `the VP Sales search` · `pilot to enterprise` · `closing the loop`
Bad: `Quick Question` · `Scaling Your Sales Team` · `Idea for [Company]` · anything with a
company name in it

## Personalization depth

Three tiers. Match the effort to the score.

| Score | Depth |
|---|---|
| 70+ | Rewrite the opening two lines entirely from research. Reference something specific — a product detail, a market they just entered, the actual title on the job posting. Consider a video or voice note on touch 3. |
| 55–69 | Replace the opening line with the verified signal. Keep the rest of the play intact. |
| 40–54 | The play as written, correct name and vertical variant. One touch only. |

## The self-check

Before staging any draft, answer these. If any answer is no, rewrite.

1. Is the opening observation **verifiably true**? Did I actually see the posting, the date,
   the page?
2. Could this message have been sent to a different company with only the name changed?
   (If yes, it isn't personalized — fix it.)
3. Does it contain anything from the kill list?
4. Is it under 150 words?
5. Does the insight say something a SaaS-generalist fractional operator could not credibly say?
6. Is there a clean, dignified way for them to decline?

## What to flag for the human

Always surface, never quietly guess:

- Any observation you could not fully verify
- A contact email you inferred rather than confirmed
- A company where the vertical fit is arguable
- Any message where you departed meaningfully from the play
- Anything where the founder's name or pronouns were unclear from sources — use their full
  name rather than guessing a short form, and write around pronouns entirely
