# The plays

Five plays, each anchored to a different situation. Each carries a digital health and a
construction tech variant. **The bracketed sections are yours to replace with real research —
everything else is structure and domain insight that should survive mostly intact.**

Selection logic:

| Signal that fired | Play |
|---|---|
| 1 — hiring AEs, no sales leader | Play 1 |
| 2 or 4 — stale VP req, or first hire departed | Play 2 |
| 3, 5, or 6 only — no hiring signal | Play 3 |
| Target is a VC/PE platform partner, not a company | Play 4 |
| A warm path exists to the target | Play 5 — always prefer this over cold |

## Play 2 — The reframe on why the search is stuck

**Requires: a req open 60+ days, or a departed first sales hire.**

### Digital health

> Subject: the VP Sales search
>
> [Name] —
>
> Your VP of Sales role has been open a while. That's usually one of three things: comp doesn't
> clear what a health-tech VP gets elsewhere, the JD is asking one person to be both the first
> rep and the first leader, or there's nothing built yet for a VP to inherit.
>
> The third is worth naming, because it's also why your best candidates are passing. A strong VP
> asks what the process is, what the ICP is, what's actually in the CRM — and when the honest
> answer is "the founders," they take the other offer. You're competing for people who have
> options.
>
> I do the six months before that search. Build the engine, then run the search against a real
> scorecard and a real system for someone to walk into. 23 years operating, quotas to $12M,
> digital health throughout.
>
> Worth 20 minutes?

>
> [Name] —
>
> That role's been open a while. In contech it's usually the same bind: the software sales
> leaders who apply have never sold to a GC, and the construction people who know the buyer have

## Play 3 — The diagnostic question

**No signal required. Leads on domain insight alone. Give them a clean way to say no.**

### Digital health

> Subject: pilot to enterprise
>
> [Name] —
>
> No pitch in this one. I'm building a short list of digital health companies I'd want to work
> with and yours is on it.
>
> The question I'd ask if we spoke: what share of your pilots have converted to enterprise
> agreements, and how long did the ones that converted actually take? That ratio tends to be the
> honest measure of whether a company has a sales process or a founder with very good
> relationships. Nearly every digital health company I've worked with hit a wall there somewhere
> between $2M and $6M — the pilots keep coming, the conversions don't, and it takes a year to
> see it clearly in the numbers.
>
> 23 years selling, quotas to $12M. I spend six months building the engine — ICP, narrative,
> cadences, process, CRM — then hire the VP of Sales who takes it over.
>
> If that ratio is already where you want it, ignore me entirely. If it isn't, worth 20 minutes.

>

## Follow-up sequence

Four touches over 18 days, then stop. Touch 1 is the play above.

| Touch | Day | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Email | The play |
| 2 | 4 | LinkedIn connect + note | Two lines. Reference the same observation, don't repeat the pitch. |
| 3 | 11 | Email | One new piece of value — a relevant observation, a comp benchmark, something you saw in their market. Not "circling back". For top-20 targets, a 45-second video or voice note instead. |
| 4 | 18 | Email | The close-out, below. |

### Touch 4 — the close-out

> Subject: closing the loop
>
> [Name] —
>
> Closing the loop on this one. If founder-led sales is still where your week goes six months
> from now, I'm easy to find. If it isn't, that's the better outcome.

No guilt, no final-attempt framing. Replies about one time in six, and leaves the door open.
