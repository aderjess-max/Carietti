# Channel 2 — Articles and blogs (employee-facing content library)

**Audience**: Employees, family members, and people at every point on the sobriety spectrum —
newly sober, sober curious, in long-term recovery, supporting a loved one, or just beginning to
ask questions.

**The posture that matters**: this person came here voluntarily. They are ready for information,
not a lecture.

**Cadence**: 3–4 articles or blogs per month.

## Format

Every article follows this exact structure.

| Field | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Title** | A clear, searchable question or statement. 6–10 words. Plain language. |
| **3 subheadings** | Short, scannable section headers breaking the topic into three parts. |
| **P1** | Answers the first subheading. 3–5 sentences. Warm, plain, practical. |
| **P2** | Answers the second subheading. 3–5 sentences. Can include steps or things to consider. |
| **P3** | Answers the third subheading. 3–5 sentences. Always ends with a sense of agency — what can the reader do next? |

## The 5 content pillars

| Pillar | Who it serves |
|---|---|
| **Understanding & Awareness** | What SUD is, how it shows up, what it means. Educational without being clinical, always without shame. |
| **Recovery & Journey** | Practical guidance for people navigating their own sobriety at work and in life. |
| **Supporting Someone You Love** | Partners, parents, friends, and family members trying to help. |
| **Workplace & Practical Tools** | Managers, HR teams, and employees navigating SUD support in professional settings. |
| **Sober Curious & Low-Stigma Entry Points** | Non-crisis content for people beginning to explore their relationship with substances. |

Language rules in `SKILL.md` apply here without exception, and this channel is where the "single
right path" failure shows up most. Never write as though abstinence is the only destination or
crisis is the only entry point.
