# Channel 3 — 1:1 outreach email (Jess writing as Sobrynth)

Direct email to prospects, channel partners, and press. Distinct from the LinkedIn and article
channels, and distinct from the fractional practice's operator voice in
`.claude/skills/prospect/references/voice.md`. **When writing Sobrynth outreach, this file wins.**

## The gold standard

Her own final draft, SIIA conference outreach to a group captive CRO, 2026-08-18. Match this.

> Hi Kevin,
>
> I saw you're speaking at SIIA in October and, rather than trying to catch you onsite when
> everyone's running in 10 directions, I figured I'd reach out now.
>
> I think a lot about groups of your size and the claims risk you carry.
>
> Untreated substance use disorder costs employers roughly $8,817 per affected employee each year.
> In knowing that 1 in 10 people in the US are affected by SUD and 70% of people with SUD are
> actively employed, that number adds up.
>
> That's exactly the exposure we're focused on at Sobrynth, and why I think what we're building
> would be incredibly relevant to you and your team.
>
> Can I steal 20 minutes of your time before SIIA? I'd genuinely value your take.
>
> Jess

## The five beats

1. **The hook and the reason for timing.** Name the thing that puts them on her radar, then say why
   now instead of later. One sentence, not two.
2. **The positioning line.** A single standalone sentence that says she already thinks about people
   like them. "I think a lot about groups of your size and the claims risk you carry." This is the
   line that separates her from a blast, and it does more work than any compliment would.
3. **The data.** Two or three stats, stacked so the third one makes the first one bigger.
4. **The bridge to Sobrynth.** Connect the exposure she just described to what Sobrynth does.
   Short. Not a pitch.
5. **The ask, framed as a small favor**, and a deferential close.

## Voice markers

- **One to two sentences per paragraph.** Generous white space. Every paragraph break is deliberate.
- **Sentences flow.** Commas and conjunctions, not clipped fragments. She is warm, not hard-boiled.
  Do not write her as a terse operator — that is the practice voice, not this one.
- **No em dashes.** She doesn't use them. Use commas, periods, or a new paragraph.
- **No exclamation points**, even though her instinct reaches for one. She cuts them.
- **First person and self-aware**: "I figured I'd," "I think a lot about," "I'd genuinely value."
- **Polite softeners carry the ask**: "rather than," "Can I steal," "I'd love to."
- **Enthusiasm adverbs, sparingly**: "incredibly," "genuinely." One or two per email, never more.
- **Length: 120–160 words.** Her final ran 135.

## Banned

- "worth a conversation" / "worth a chat" / any **"worth a ___"** construction. Reads as
  AI-generated on sight. Her call, 2026-08-18.
- The full outreach kill list in `.claude/skills/prospect/references/voice.md` applies here too:
  no "I hope this finds you well," no "just reaching out," no "circling back," no "quick question,"
  no consultant-speak, no stat without a source.
- Person-first language rules in `SKILL.md` apply without exception.

## Subject lines

Lowercase, three to five words, reads like a colleague's internal email. No company names, no
exclamation points.

Default: `before October` (or whatever the timing hook is). Sharper when the subject can prove she
knows their work: `downstream of a FibroScan` · `what's inside your large claims` ·
`the EAP line item`.

## Stat discipline

**Unresolved as of 2026-08-18 — pin this down before the next send.** The $8,817 figure appears two
ways across her materials:

- `SKILL.md` (from the brand guidelines): "$8,817 cost to employers **per capita** annually"
- Her outreach draft: "$8,817 **per affected employee** each year"

These differ by roughly an order of magnitude, and the self-insured audience does exactly this math
for a living. Commonly circulated versions of the figure describe cost per employee *with* an
untreated substance use disorder, which supports the outreach phrasing — but that has not been
verified against the primary SAMHSA/NSC source. **Verify once, then standardize the wording
everywhere.** Do not let the two phrasings coexist.

Same check on "1 in 10": the brand stat set says 1 in 10 *employees* is actively struggling with
untreated SUD. "1 in 10 people in the US are affected by SUD" is a broader and different claim.
