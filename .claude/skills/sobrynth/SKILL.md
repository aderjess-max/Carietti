## Who Sobrynth is

**Mission**: We help employees figure out their sobriety journey so they feel safer at home and at work.

**Vision**: A world where people are supported, not stigmatized — at home and at work — on their
journey with substance use disorder. We meet people where they are without judgment. There is no
right or wrong path. We help people navigate the path they choose.

**What Sobrynth provides**: 24/7 assistance for employees and their families seeking support with
substance use disorder needs, and for employees who are sober curious. Sobrynth also helps employers
build recovery-friendly workplace cultures through a 90-day cultural change program, peer recovery
coaches, ERG frameworks, and a support platform — positioned as an alternative to traditional EAP
programs.

**Core values**: Employee First · Empathy · Integrity · Trust · Bravery.

**Tagline**: Real Support for Substance Use. Built for your Workforce.
**Positioning shorthand**: Substance agnostic. Acuity agnostic.

### The core message

This belongs in every piece of content in some form:

> Sobriety is personal. Whatever your relationship with substances looks like — total sobriety,
> recovery, sober curious, cutting back, California sober, or supporting someone you love —
> Sobrynth is here. No judgment. No pressure. Just support, whenever you're ready.

## Brand voice

Every piece of content must be **compassionate, confident, and clear**.

**Compassionate** — We meet people where they are. Write as though sitting across from someone who
is finally ready to talk, and we're honored they came. No shame. No lecture. No assumption about
where they are in their journey.

**Confident** — We don't hedge. We don't over-explain. We state facts plainly. Recovery-friendly
workplaces are good business. Substance use disorder is the most stigmatized and underserved
condition in the American workforce. These are facts, and we say so.

**Clear** — No jargon. No clinical language unless it is immediately explained. Short sentences.
Plain words. Concrete next steps. Write like a smart, caring human — not like a benefits brochure.

## Non-negotiable language rules

Person-first, stigma-free language. Every piece, no exceptions.

| ❌ Never use | ✅ Always use instead |
|---|---|
| Addict / Alcoholic / Drug abuser | Person with a substance use disorder |
| Substance abuse | Substance use or substance misuse |
| Clean (as in "getting clean") | In recovery / on their journey |
| Addiction (in formal content) | Substance use disorder (SUD) |
| Failing a drug test | Testing positive on a drug screen |
| Medication assisted treatment (MAT) | Medication for substance use disorder |
| Rock bottom | Their moment of choice / their turning point |
| Sober (as a fixed endpoint) | On their journey (unspecified endpoint) |

**Audience served — always the full spectrum.** People in active recovery, people newly sober,
sober curious individuals, people who want to cut back, California sober, family members of people
with SUD, allies and supporters, managers who want to help, and HR leaders building
recovery-friendly cultures. Content must never feel like it is only for people in crisis or only
for people who are fully abstinent.

## Key statistics

Source: SAMHSA. Cite the stat, not a link, in LinkedIn body copy.

- **48.5 million** Americans have substance use disorder
- **70%** of people with SUD are employed
- **1 in 10** employees is actively struggling with untreated SUD
- **2 in 3** families are impacted by SUD
- **$8,817** cost to employers per capita annually for untreated SUD — a **30% increase** over the past 3 years
- **65%** of workplace injuries in construction are linked to SUD
- **21 million** Americans are in recovery

The construction stat is load-bearing. It connects to the construction wedge in `CLAUDE.md`:
construction has the highest overdose mortality rate of any US occupation (162.6 per 100k), 12%
alcohol use disorder against a 7.5% national rate, and 150% higher SUD diagnosis rates. Highest-need,
most underserved employer segment in the country.

## Banned phrasing

Applies to every Sobrynth email, post, and article.

- **"worth a conversation" / "worth a chat" / "worth exploring" / any "worth a ___" construction.**
  Reads as AI-generated on sight and undoes the voice. Her call, 2026-08-18. Make the ask plainly
  instead: "Any chance you have twenty minutes?"

The full outreach kill list lives in `.claude/skills/prospect/references/voice.md` and applies to
Sobrynth email as well.

## Quality check — run before every output

1. Does this feel compassionate, confident, and clear?
2. Does it use person-first language throughout?
3. Does it serve the full spectrum of the audience — not only people in active crisis or full abstinence?
4. Does it sound like a trusted, informed human — not a corporation or a clinician?
5. Does it end with something for the reader to hold onto?
6. Does it carry the core message — sobriety is personal, and Sobrynth is here for all of it — in some form?

If any answer is no, revise before delivering.

## Two voices, one operator

Jess writes in two registers and they are not interchangeable.

- **Sobrynth brand voice** (this skill) — trusted informed friend, person-first language, hashtags,
  the core message in every piece.
- **Jess's own practice voice** (`CLAUDE.md` → LinkedIn and content) — 23-year operator, direct
  ask, no hashtags on personal posts, URLs in the first comment.

When she posts on her own LinkedIn profile *about* Sobrynth, the **format rules stay hers** (no
hashtags, native post, URL in the first comment) and the **language rules stay Sobrynth's**
(person-first, full spectrum, no stigmatizing terms). The hashtag set in `references/linkedin.md`
is for Sobrynth-channel posts. Ask if it is ever unclear which channel a piece is for.

*Sobriety is personal. So is every word we write.*
