# Boston Children's Hospital — Sobrynth

**Alignment call:** 2026-09-21 (today), Head of Benefits, 1:1
**Group meeting:** 2026-09-29, in person, wider committee

Purpose of today's call: find out what the group on the 29th actually needs, and make the
Head of Benefits look good in front of them. He is not the audience for a pitch today — he is
the internal champion, and today is about arming him.

---

## 0. What we're selling into (context, not to be recited)

Sobrynth is a workplace SUD benefit: 24/7 peer recovery coaching, concierge care navigation,
a 90-day Recovery-Friendly Workplace activation, employees *and* families, no diagnosis
required, covering sober-curious through active use through long-term recovery. The wedge
against the incumbent stack is utilization: EAPs run 2–5% overall and under 1% of that is SUD,
and peer support — the single most effective bridge into treatment — is almost never in the
EAP model.

**Why a pediatric academic medical center is not a generic employer logo.** In healthcare, the
barrier to disclosure is not stigma in the abstract — it is licensure. A nurse or physician who
raises a hand fears the Board, not the water cooler. Every other employer segment's "confidential
benefit" pitch lands differently here, and that is the conversation that will decide the 29th.
Second: hospitals already have a controlled-substance diversion program, and today it almost
always terminates in discipline and a report, not treatment and retention. That is the most
expensive unaddressed problem in the building and nobody owns it as a benefits problem.

**Assumptions to verify, not assert** (all of these are the kind of thing to ask, never state):
BCH is roughly 9–10k+ employees, likely self-insured, nurses represented by the MNA, residents
and fellows on separate benefit structures, and MA has established professional monitoring
programs (SARP for nurses, Physician Health Services for physicians). Confirm each on the call
rather than demonstrating homework that turns out to be wrong.

## 1. Today's call — the eight questions that matter

Keep it to 20 minutes and let him talk. These are in priority order; if you get through five,
you got what you came for.

**The room**

1. Who's going to be in the room on the 29th, and what does each of them own? Specifically — is
   anyone coming from nursing leadership, employee health, occupational health, risk, or legal?
2. Of those people, who is the hardest to convince, and what's their objection going to be?
3. Who actually signs off if this moves forward — you, or someone who isn't in the room?

**The decision**

4. What's the decision on the 29th? Are we deciding whether this category is worth pursuing,
   whether to pilot, or whether it goes into the FY plan? And what's the gate after that one?
5. What made this a live conversation now? Was there an incident, a survey result, a renewal
   coming up, something from the exec team, or has this been sitting on your list for a while?

**The ground truth**

6. What's in place today for substance use specifically — the EAP, the health plan's SUD
   network, anything on the pharmacy side — and what does the utilization actually look like?
7. When a nurse or a physician here has a substance use problem, what happens today? Walk me
   through the real path, not the policy. Where does it go through employee health, and where
   does it go through the diversion program?

**The ask**

8. What do you need from me on the 29th to make this easy for you? If I bring one thing that
   makes your case for you, what should it be — the clinical model, the utilization data, the
   economics, or the licensure and confidentiality architecture?

**Close with:** "What's the one thing I could say on the 29th that would cost me the room?"
That question is worth the whole call.

## 2. Bank for the 29th — full discovery

Don't run all of these. Pick from the banks the alignment call points at.

### A. Population and exposure

- How many employees, and how does that split between clinical, research, and administrative?
- How much of the workforce is nights, rotating, or 12-hour shifts? ED, ICU, OR, anesthesia?
- Are dependents and spouses in scope for benefits like this, or employees only?
- Which populations are on separate benefit structures — residents and fellows, per diem,
  travelers, research staff on grant funding?
- Which groups are union-represented, and does a benefit like this have to be bargained or
  can it be offered outside the contract?

### B. The current stack

- Who's the EAP, when does that contract renew, and what's the utilization?
- What share of EAP volume is substance use as opposed to stress, family, or financial?
- Is there a behavioral health point solution already in place, and does its scope include SUD
  or stop at therapy and coaching?
- On the medical side — what does the plan cover for residential, IOP, and MAT? Is MAT
  carved out to pharmacy, and is there prior auth on buprenorphine?
- What are you seeing in SUD claims spend, and how much of it is out-of-network?
- Who does care navigation today when someone needs residential treatment, and how long does
  it take them to get placed?

### C. The healthcare-specific problem (this is the one)

- What does your employee population believe happens if they disclose a substance use problem?
  Not what the policy says — what do they believe?
- How does the licensure question shape this? Is there an established relationship with the
  state monitoring programs for nurses and physicians, and how is that path experienced —
  as a lifeline or as the end of a career?
- Tell me about the diversion program. How many cases a year, and what's the disposition?
  How many of those people are still employed here twelve months later?
- Where does fitness-for-duty sit, and who makes the return-to-work call?
- What are managers trained to do when someone comes to them? What are they trained *not* to do?
- Has there been a case in the last two years that the organization still talks about?
- If an employee needed 30 days of residential treatment, what happens to their job, their
  license, and their schedule? Is there a path that ends with them back at work?

### D. Economics and what counts as a win

- What does it cost you to replace a staff nurse, all in? A physician?
- Do you have a view of what substance use is costing the organization today — claims,
  absence, turnover, overtime backfill, or incidents?
- What metric would make this obviously worth it to the person you named as hardest to convince?
- Where would the money come from — benefits, workforce and retention, quality and safety, or
  a wellbeing budget? Are those different approvers?
- What have you bought recently that worked, and what's the story you tell about why it worked?

### E. Process, timing and the path to live

- What's the plan year, and when's the renewal that this would have to land against?
- Is there a benefits consultant or broker involved, and do they need to be in this?
- What does the procurement path look like — RFP, sole source, or pilot under a threshold?
- Walk me through the security and privacy review. BAA, InfoSec assessment, vendor risk — how
  long does that take here and who owns it?
- Is a pilot possible — one population, one unit, a defined window — or does this have to be
  enterprise from day one?
- Realistically, from a yes on the 29th to employees having access: how long?

### F. Surfacing the objection before it surfaces itself

- What's the argument against doing this, from the person best positioned to make it?
- Has anyone here tried something in this space before that didn't work? What happened?
- What would make you personally regret bringing this to the group?

## 3. What to bring on the 29th

Decide after today's call. The default kit, in priority order:

1. **The confidentiality and licensure architecture** — on one page. Who sees what, what is
   never reported to the employer, how it interacts with the Board and the monitoring programs.
   In a hospital this is the first question and the whole deal. Have it in writing.
2. **The utilization argument** — EAP at 2–5% overall and under 1% SUD, against what Sobrynth
   sees. Their own EAP numbers, if he gives them, make this argument for you.
3. **The diversion-to-treatment path** — the retention story. What the current path costs them
   in replaced clinicians and what an alternative looks like.
4. **Families** — no diagnosis required, spouse and dependent access. In a pediatric hospital
   this lands differently than anywhere else.
5. **One artifact he can forward** after the meeting without you in the room.

**Do not bring:** a generic employer deck, a national statistics opener, or anything with an
ROI number that you can't defend line by line in front of a CFO's analyst.
