## The situation this engagement is built for

Philanthropy has funded the product and proved it works. Nothing yet converts a pilot user into recurring earned revenue.

Science to People has done the hard part. VeriSci runs on a proprietary engine drawing from a closed, citable corpus of more than 10 million peer-reviewed and expert-authored sources, including content from the National Academy of Medicine. Two products have been through funded pilots with named institutional partners — VeriComms for public health departments, backed by the de Beaumont Foundation with the Public Health Communications Collaborative engaging health department communications staff, and Akari for credible creators, piloted with The Jed Foundation.

What is missing is everything between a successful pilot and a signed contract: a defined buyer, a price, a pitch, a pipeline, and someone whose job is to work it. That is the gap this engagement closes.

**Two products, two entirely different buying motions.** VeriComms sells to institutions — health departments, public health associations, academic centers, health systems. Longer cycles, committee decisions, procurement, but real contract value and multi-year stickiness. Akari sells to creators, and the version of Akari worth a sales leader's time is not the individual creator but the organization that sponsors a cohort of them. The JED pilot is the template for that motion, not an exception to it.

**Recommendation: sequence, don't split.** At 15 hours a week across 13 weeks, building two full commercial motions in parallel produces two half-built ones. I recommend leading with VeriComms — higher contract value, a clearly identifiable budget owner, an existing funded pilot to reference, and a distribution partner already engaged — and treating Akari as the secondary track: positioning, pricing, and a targeted org-sponsored pipeline, without the full asset build. If you would rather weight it the other way, that is a straightforward change and I would rather make it in week one than month two.

**Why Q4 is the right window and not a quiet quarter.** Public health budget planning for the next fiscal year happens in the fall, so the conversations that determine whether Science to People is a line item start now. Institutional buyers commit against calendar year-end. And both pilot cohorts are warm at this moment — the health department communicators who used VeriComms and the founding creators who used Akari are the warmest pipeline the organization will ever have, and warm cohorts cool.

## Responsibilities

Four workstreams, running in sequence but overlapping. I carry the bag on all four — this is an operating role, not an advisory one.

**1. Commercial diagnostic and positioning.** Interview leadership and pilot participants to establish what people will actually pay for, who controls that budget, and how the purchase gets approved. Define the ideal customer profile and segmentation for each product, and set pricing and packaging. Build the sales narrative on the provenance story — a closed, citable corpus and named institutional content partners — because that is the defensible difference against a general-purpose AI tool, and it is the thing a risk-averse public health buyer needs to hear first.

**2. Pipeline infrastructure.** Select and stand up the CRM, define pipeline stages with entry and exit criteria that mean the same thing to everyone, build the reporting view, and load a named target account list. Small organizations lose deals to forgetting, not to losing. This is the part that prevents that.

**3. Origination and direct selling.** Run outbound to named accounts. Take the discovery calls myself. Build and issue proposals and pilot agreements. Work the warm cohorts — the health departments that used VeriComms, the founding creators from the Akari pilot, and the funder and partner networks around both — before working cold ones. Map the channel partners that give a small team reach it cannot buy: the Public Health Communications Collaborative, national and state public health associations, schools of public health, and the funders themselves.

**4. Systematization and handoff.** Document every play, template, and decision so the motion runs after December 31. Write the 2027 revenue plan. Recommend the first full-time commercial hire — the role, the profile, the scorecard, the comp range, and when to pull the trigger. I am building something that outlasts the engagement, and I will know I got it right if by January you can name your buyer, hold your price, and work your next fifty accounts without me in the room.

## What success looks like

Success in a 13-week engagement is a working pipeline and the system that produced it — not closed revenue, and I want to be direct about why.

Institutional public health procurement commonly runs three to nine months from first conversation to signature. An engagement that ends December 31 cannot be honestly measured on booked revenue without either sandbagging the target or pushing toward small, badly priced deals that set a damaging precedent. The first price Science to People charges becomes the price it is compared against for years. I would rather leave you with a well-qualified pipeline at a defensible price than a signed contract at a number you regret.

| Measure | Target by December 31 | Why this one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified discovery conversations | 25–35 | The input everything else depends on; also the fastest way to learn what the market will pay |
| Qualified opportunities | 8–12 | Budget owner identified, need confirmed, timeline named — not just interest |
| Proposals or pilot agreements issued | 4–6 | Proves the pitch, pricing, and paper actually work under live conditions |
| Signed agreements or letters of intent | 1–3 | Deliberately modest; anything above this is upside, not the plan |
| Qualified pipeline carried into Q1 2027 | Set jointly in week 2, once pricing is fixed | A dollar target set before pricing exists is a guess; this one gets a real number as soon as it can have one |
| Infrastructure | All October–December deliverables shipped, CRM live, 100% of activity logged | The part that compounds after I leave |

**The qualitative test.** By January, Science to People can name its buyer, defend its price, and work its next fifty accounts without me. If that is true and the numbers above are met, the engagement worked. If the numbers are met and that is not true, it did not.

**Reviewed monthly, adjusted openly.** These targets are set before I have seen your list quality, your warm cohort response rates, or your true cycle length. I will bring a recommendation to revise them at the end of October, when I have real data instead of assumptions. Targets that quietly stay wrong for three months help nobody.

## Out of scope, and what I need from you

Fifteen hours a week is roughly two days. Naming the boundary is what keeps those two days productive.

**Not included in this engagement:** grant writing or philanthropic fundraising; marketing content production, brand, or design; product management and roadmap; board or investor materials beyond the 2027 revenue plan; managing staff or contractors; and full-time, on-call, or same-day-response coverage.

**What I need from Science to People:**

- 60 minutes a week with the CEO, on a standing slot
- Warm introductions to 6–10 pilot participants inside the first two weeks — this is the single biggest determinant of whether October lands
- Budget for the CRM; HubSpot and Salesforce both offer substantial nonprofit pricing, and I will bring a recommendation in week one
- A sciencetopeople.org email address with its sending domain authenticated and warmed before outbound begins in November
- Named decision authority on pricing by October 15, so the pricing recommendation has somewhere to land
- Access to pilot outcome data and any evaluation findings, which are the proof points the pitch will be built on
