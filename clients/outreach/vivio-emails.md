# VIVIO outreach — pharmacy consultants + CFOs

Drafted 2026-08-25. Both written to the voice rules in
`.claude/skills/prospect/references/voice.md`: under 150 words, one rhetorical question
max, no kill-list language, a clean way to decline.

---

## 1. Pharmacy consultants — Mercer, Aon, WTW, Segal

Touch 2. Follows a short intro email.

**Subject:** the 7-Eleven number

> Hi [Name] —
>
> $34M. That's what VIVIO took out of 7-Eleven's pharmacy spend last year, and it's the
> number I didn't have room for last time.
>
> How matters more than how much. No alternative funding. No patient assistance programs,
> no charity foundations, none of the ERISA exposure you've spent years steering clients
> away from. VIVIO buys on the open market and earns one thing: a flat admin fee. Nothing
> in our revenue moves when a client's drug spend moves.
>
> No formulary either. Members don't lose access to a drug so the savings can work. Trial
> data and the patient's own chart decide, not a rebate schedule.
>
> That's a recommendation that holds up in a fiduciary review instead of one you have to
> hedge.
>
> Twenty minutes? I'll bring the Validation Institute certification and the fee structure.
> If specialty isn't live in your book right now, say so and I'll stay out of your way.
>
> Jess

**Why it's built this way.** A pharmacy consultant's real fear isn't cost, it's
recommending a vendor that blows up on them. They have spent years warning clients off
alternative funding programs — charity-shifting, ERISA and IRS exposure, the model Drug
Channels called shady. Every carve-out pitch that reaches their desk is one of those.
Leading with what VIVIO *isn't* separates from the category before they file it. The
flat-admin-fee line answers "how do you make money," which is the second thing they ask
and the thing that disqualifies most vendors. "No formulary" closes the last door —
they can't be accused of trading member access for savings. The close offers
substantiation rather than a demo, which is what a consultant actually needs.

## 2. CFOs

Touch 2. Follows the intro email that led with the $34M and offered a claims analysis.

**Subject:** where the spend actually sits

> Jeff —
>
> One more number for you. Specialty drugs are under 2% of the prescriptions written in
> this country. They're about half of what gets spent on drugs. Your plan almost certainly
> looks like that.
>
> That concentration is why $34M was sitting there at 7-Eleven. The savings weren't spread
> across thousands of claims. They were in a few hundred.
>
> It also means this isn't a benefits redesign. Nobody's plan changes, nobody loses a
> medication, and it plugs into the carriers and PBM you already have.
>
> The claims analysis costs nothing and runs on about an hour of your team's time. You get
> the number either way, and it's a useful thing to have in hand before a fiduciary review
> asks you for it.
>
> Fifteen minutes, or tell me it's not a priority this year and I'll close the file.
>
> Jess

**Why it's built this way.** The first email was a savings pitch, and a CFO gets ten of
those a week. Repeating it louder does nothing. This one reframes: the concentration fact
is genuinely new information and it makes the problem look *tractable* — a few hundred
claims, not a plan overhaul. That reframe is what makes the free analysis feel small
enough to say yes to. The fiduciary line converts a cost conversation into a personal-risk
one, which is the register that actually moves a CFO who ignored a savings number. "I'll
close the file" gives a dignified exit and signals there are other accounts.

## Notes before sending

- **Verify the 7-Eleven figure and the naming permission.** $34M from Jess, from inside
  the account. It isn't in any public VIVIO material I could find. Naming a customer plus
  a specific savings number in cold outbound usually needs reference clearance — worth a
  check with VIVIO marketing if it hasn't happened, since it now appears in two email
  templates rather than one.
- **The <2% / ~50% split** is the national figure (~$500B total US drug spend, ~$250B
  specialty). Written as a national pattern on purpose. Don't tighten it into a claim
  about the recipient's own plan.
- **Validation Institute certification** is 39% lower PMPM year-over-year, from a
  2021-vs-2022 study of one employer. Confirm it's been renewed before offering it as
  current proof in the consultant email.
- **Consultant variant**: if the recipient's firm has its own PBM coalition arrangement,
  cut the fiduciary line. It reads as an accusation rather than an alignment.
