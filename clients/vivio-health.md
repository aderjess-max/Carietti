## What it sells

Two products, one thesis:

| Product | Scope | Savings claim |
|---|---|---|
| **VIVIO Precision Care™** | Specialty drug carve-out — pharmacy *and* medical benefit drugs | 20–30% (mid/large self-insured employers); other collateral says 30–50%, and 30–40% of current specialty spend |
| **PrecisionCare One™** | Full PBM replacement — specialty, non-specialty, medical; includes a GLP-1 clinical management program | 15–25% annually for a typical mid-sized or large self-insured employer |

PrecisionCare One launched **Nov 25, 2024** and is positioned as "the first full PBM
replacement platform." First named customer: **Lake County Schools** — quote from their
Director of Risk Management, Robert Oyer: *"We were tired of playing the rebate shell
game."* That quote is the whole PBM-replacement pitch in nine words; it travels.

Underneath both: **VIVIO qClinicals™**, built in-house — clinical trial data for
specialty conditions (Crohn's, cancer, HIV, MS, hep C, oncology, rheumatology) matched
against individual patient data from EMRs, plus market prices *including cash prices*.
"Right drug, right patient, right price."

## The mechanics a buyer will ask about

- VIVIO carves the specialty benefit out of the PBM and/or health plan and **becomes the
  primary payor** for drugs on the plan's Specialty Drug List.
- **It plugs into the employer's existing carriers and PBMs** — this is the answer to
  "do I have to rip out my stack."
- **No formulary.** Any treatment with supporting evidence can be considered. This is the
  deliberate inversion of the PBM model (which excludes drugs and restricts access) and
  it's the reason the clinical story and the cost story are the same story, not a
  trade-off. Lead with it — it defuses the "carve-outs restrict my people" objection
  before it's raised.
- **Independent**: owns no providers and no pharmacies.
- **Revenue is an administrative fee only** — no spread, no rebate economics, no upside
  from utilization or drug price. Say this early; it's the trust unlock.
- **Employers own their data**, with unfettered access to VIVIO and market data.

## The Cuban partnership — how to actually use it

VIVIO sources specialty drugs through Mark Cuban Cost Plus Drug Company. Origin story:
Cuban mentioned on The Daily Show that a friend was quoted **$3,000/month** for generic
droxidopa; Cost Plus sourced the same drug at **$75 for a three-month supply** — under 1%
of the price. Covered by Managed Healthcare Executive, Becker's Payer Issues, Benzinga,
PRWeb.

Sales value: it is instant name recognition and instant credibility on the "open market
pricing is real, not theoretical" claim — with a benefits audience that has heard a
hundred savings pitches. Use it as the *illustration* of the model, never as the model
itself. The product is the clinical platform; Cuban is a supply channel and a door-opener.

## Market framing / the macro pitch

- US spends **~$500B/yr** on prescription drugs. **<2% of prescriptions are specialty —
  and they account for ~$250B, half of total drug spend.** That's the whole reason a
  carve-out is a board-level line item and not a benefits-team nit.
- **CAA fiduciary exposure is the timing argument.** The Consolidated Appropriations Act
  imposed fiduciary-prudence and transparency obligations on group health plan sponsors,
  and an expected wave of breach-of-fiduciary-duty litigation over vendor selection and
  monitoring is already visible in the legal press. VIVIO's homepage leads on **"No
  Fiduciary Risk"** — lowest net cost, full data ownership, no hidden vendor economics.
  For a benefits leader or CFO, this converts a cost-savings conversation into a
  personal-risk conversation, which closes faster.
- **GLP-1s** are the current cost shock and PrecisionCare One's clinical management
  program is the wedge into accounts that aren't ready to talk about a full carve-out yet.

## Competitive set

Specialty carve-out vendors: **Archimedes, Nirvana Health, PaydHealth, PriceMDs,
RxResults, ImpaxRX, SHARx, PayerMatrix, Script Sourcing.** Adjacent PBM-alternative
players (SmithRx et al.) become direct competitors on PrecisionCare One deals.

**The differentiation that matters, and it's a real one:** most "specialty carve-out"
vendors are *alternative funding programs* — they shift drug cost onto manufacturer
patient assistance programs and charities. Drug Channels has called that model shady;
AIS Health has run industry criticism of it; BCBS plans publish warnings about it; it
carries ERISA and IRS compliance exposure for the employer.

VIVIO is not that. It buys on the open market and takes an admin fee. **Expect every
prospect to have been burned by, or warned about, an AFP vendor — so lead by naming the
category's bad actors and separating from them.** "We don't touch patient assistance
programs" is a stronger opener than any savings number, because it answers the
suspicion the buyer already walked in with. Prospects and their brokers will lump VIVIO
in with AFPs unless told not to.
