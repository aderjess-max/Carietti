Fractional VP of Sales · Launch Playbook · Week of 18 August 2026

The Origination Engine

Where the outreach goes, how often, in what form, and to whom — starting Monday. Everything here is built to be executed alongside your current 1099 work, not after it.

target 5 signings 25 actions / week 3 conversations / week fully remote verticals: health + construction
01

What remote changes

Working fully remote removes your geographic ceiling and raises the cost of being a generalist. Those are the same fact seen from two sides.

When you sell within an hour's drive, proximity does some of your differentiation for you — you're the operator who shows up. Nationally, you're one of roughly 9,000 fractional sales leaders, and the founder comparing you to three others on a Zoom has nothing to go on but what you claim to know. That's why the two-vertical wedge matters more now, not less.

So the model is a beachhead, not a boundary:

  • NY metro is where you start — your 23-year network is here, in-person coffee still converts better than a cold Zoom, and being able to say "I can be in your office Thursday" closes deals that remote competitors can't reach. Work this hard for the first two clients.
  • Vertical is where you scale — digital health and construction tech founders in Denver, Austin, and Boston have the same problem and fewer credible options. Every case study you publish travels nationally; every referral from a VC platform partner travels nationally.

Practically: keep "NYC metro" in your copy, drop it from your filters. Say you're NY-based and work with companies nationally. The location signals substance to the local network and costs you nothing with a founder in Chicago.

02

The volume math

Five signings is the year's number. Working backward through a realistic funnel, weighted toward warm and referred sources because that's where your leverage is:

~1,200 Total outreach actions across the year, all channels
150 Real conversations that result
50 Qualified calls — real problem, real budget, real timing
5 Signed engagements, via ~15 proposals

Divided across 48 working weeks that's roughly 25 outbound actions, 3 conversations, and 1 qualified call per week. Modest volume by the standards of what you've managed — but it has to happen every week, including the ones where delivery is loud.

Channel Weekly actions Expected reply Deals
Warm network reactivation 5–20 50–60% 1–2
VC & PE platform partners 3–5 25–35% 2–3
Signal-based cold outbound 15 8–12% 1–2
Recruiter referral swap 1–2 40%+ 1

The one rule

The deals column is a forecast; the actions column is a commitment. Never let a delivery week eat the origination block. The classic solo-practice failure is three busy months followed by an empty slot and an empty pipeline — and the gap between them is always a quarter you can't get back.

03

Channels, modality, and cadence

Warm network — your fastest path to client one

Who: 250 named people from 23 years — former colleagues, managers, reports, customers, partners, vendors. Tier them: T1 are people who would take your call today (~40), T2 know you well but haven't spoken in years (~110), T3 are looser ties (~100).

Modality: T1 gets a phone call or a coffee, not a message. T2 gets a personal email, LinkedIn DM as backup. T3 gets LinkedIn DM. No broadcast announcement post — it converts far worse than 40 individual messages and it burns the news value.

Cadence: 20/week for the first 12 weeks to clear the list, then 5/week as maintenance. Follow up once after 10 days, then leave it.

The angle: Ask for a read on your positioning, not for business. People help with advice more readily than with referrals, and the referral follows the advice conversation about a third of the time.

VC and PE platform partners — highest leverage in the plan

Who: 40 named platform, talent, or portfolio-services partners — the person at the fund whose job is helping portfolio companies hire and operate, not the investing partner. Titles: Head of Talent, Platform Director, Head of Portfolio Services, Operating Partner.

Modality: LinkedIn DM or email to book a 20-minute intro call. This is a relationship channel, not a pitch channel — the call's only goal is that they know precisely what you do and precisely who you're right for.

Cadence: 3–5 new outreaches/week until the list is worked. Then a genuinely useful quarterly touch — a market observation, a comp benchmark, a note on what you're seeing in their vertical. Not a check-in.

Why it's first: These people field "our founder can't get out of founder-led sales" constantly and have nobody good to hand it to. One trusted relationship here produces referrals for years, and referrals arrive pre-qualified and pre-trusted, which is worth more than the volume.

Signal-based cold outbound — the repeatable engine

Who: Companies showing a buying signal, not companies matching a firmographic profile. The signals are in section four and they're the whole game — untriggered cold outbound to "Series A healthtech" converts at a fraction of the rate.

Modality: A four-touch sequence over 18 days — email, LinkedIn connect with note, email, LinkedIn DM. Add a 45-second video or voice note on touch three for your top-20 targets; it roughly doubles reply rate and almost nobody at your level bothers. Then stop. A fifth touch to a founder who hasn't answered costs you the relationship for when the signal is real.

Cadence: 15 new companies entered per week, which means roughly 30–40 total sends weekly once sequences overlap. Batch the research Monday morning; let the sends run through the week.

Recruiter referral swap — the underused one

Who: Sales-focused recruiting firms and independent sales recruiters. Betts, RevPilots, Paraform's recruiter network, plus independents on LinkedIn who post VP Sales searches.

The pitch: They turn away founders every week who want a VP of Sales but aren't ready for one — no process, no comp plan, no scorecard, nothing for a VP to inherit. Placing into that produces a fall-off, which costs them the fee. That rejected pile is your exact ICP.

Be explicit about the fee overlap up front. Two clean structures: you refer the eventual search to them and waive your placement fee, or you split it. Ambiguity here turns a partner into a competitor about four months in.

Cadence: 1–2 new recruiter conversations a month, high-touch. This is a small set of relationships, not a list to blast.

Communities and content — compounding, not immediate

Communities: One vertical event or community every two weeks. Show up as an operator with a point of view, never as a vendor working the room. Pavilion is the single highest-value paid community for your peer network and referral flow; BuiltWorlds and HLTH are where your buyers are.

Content: Two to three LinkedIn posts a week, one theme only — what actually breaks when a founder hands off sales. Specific and tactical, drawn from live engagements with details changed. This produces nothing in month two and makes every other channel convert better by month six. Post from your own account; do not build a company page.

04

Buying signals — how to source targets forever

A static list of 40 companies is exhausted in six weeks. What you need is a repeatable weekly search that surfaces companies at the moment the problem becomes urgent. Ranked by how well each predicts a real conversation:

Rank Signal Why it works Where to find it
1 Hiring AEs or SDRs with no VP/Head of Sales on the team The sharpest signal there is. They're about to hand new reps a process that exists only in the founder's head. Most of those reps fail, and the founder half-knows it. LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Wellfound, Built In, YC jobs, company careers pages
2 Head of Sales or VP Sales req open more than 60 days They can't fill it — wrong comp, wrong stage, or an unsellable role. You're the bridge, and you make the eventual search fillable. Job boards, re-posted listings, LinkedIn job age
3 Series A raised 9–24 months ago Long enough to have hit the founder-led ceiling, early enough that a $250K VP still feels premature. Fresh raises are too early; 30+ months usually means they solved it or stalled. AlleyWatch, Tech:NYC Series A tracker, Crunchbase, vertical funding newsletters
4 First sales hire departed inside 18 months Proof the problem is real and the founder has now paid for it once. Painful, and therefore fundable. LinkedIn tenure checks on the company's sales alumni
5 Founder is still the contact on the demo or pricing page Literal evidence of founder-led sales. Cheap to check and surprisingly common at $3–8M. The company's own website
6 New segment, new geography, or new product announced The existing motion won't transfer, and the founder usually discovers that about 90 days in. Press releases, LinkedIn company posts, product changelogs
Your Monday morning search

Run signal 1 across both verticals every Monday: companies with 15–150 employees, posting an AE or SDR role, with no one holding a VP Sales / Head of Sales / CRO title. That single query will produce more qualified targets per week than you can work — which is exactly the position you want to be in.

The messages

Written to sound like a 23-year operator, not a consultant with a funnel. Short, specific, and asking for something small. Adapt the vertical detail per target; keep the structure.

Warm network — tier 1 & 2 Email or DM · T1 gets a call instead

Subject: quick one

[Name] —

After 23 years carrying a bag I've gone out on my own, doing one thing: I take companies stuck in founder-led sales and build the whole engine — ICP, the story, process, CRM, cadences — then hire the VP of Sales who runs it. Six months, then I'm gone.

Not pitching you. Two things I'd value:

1. If a founder comes to mind who's still the best salesperson in their own company and knows that's a problem — I'd like the intro.
2. If you've got 20 minutes, I'd genuinely like your read on how I'm positioning this.

Either way, good to be back in touch.

Asking for a read on positioning outperforms asking for referrals — and the referral tends to come out of the advice conversation anyway. One follow-up at 10 days, then stop.

VC / PE platform partner LinkedIn DM or email · goal is a 20-min call

Subject: for the portfolio companies stuck in founder-led sales

[Name] —

You probably field some version of this monthly: a founder at $2–5M still closing every deal personally, knows they need a team, not ready to commit to a $250K VP.

That's the only thing I do. Six months — I build the ICP, the narrative, the process, the CRM and the cadences, then run the search for their VP of Sales and hand the whole thing over. 23 years operating, quotas up to $12M, deepest in [health tech / construction].

The weekly rhythm

Built for the overlap period — bridge work plus building plus origination. The principle is that origination happens Monday morning before anything else touches the calendar. If it can move, it will move, and then it will vanish.

MondayAM — 3 hrs

Origination block. Immovable.

Run the signal-1 search across both verticals. Add 15 new companies to the sequence. Send 20 network reactivations (first 12 weeks) and 3–5 VC platform outreaches. Clear all pending follow-ups. Nothing else goes in this block, ever.

Mon–Thuthe bulk

Bridge delivery and asset library

Current 1099 obligations, roughly 10–20 hours. Everything left over in months 1–2 goes to the reusable asset library — ICP workbook, cadence templates, CRM build checklists, VP scorecard, comp plan templates. This is the only stretch of the year with unbooked hours; the library is what makes three concurrent clients survivable later.

WednesdayAM — held open

Discovery calls

Hold this slot open permanently and offer it first when someone says yes. Having a real time to give beats "let me check my calendar" — it's a small thing that measurably shortens the gap between reply and meeting.

FridayAM — 2 hrs

Pipeline review and follow-through

Update your own CRM — you are your first client and it should be visibly excellent. Second-touch follow-ups. Referral asks to anyone who's had a good week with you. Log what channel each conversation actually came from; by month four that attribution data should be reallocating your Monday block.

FridayPM — 1 hr

Content and community

Write the week's two LinkedIn posts. Community engagement, event scheduling. Low-stakes, done tired, still compounds.

The four numbers to track weekly

Metric Weekly target What a miss means
New outbound actions 25 Pure activity. If this misses, nothing downstream is diagnosable — fix it first.
Conversations held 3 Actions fine but conversations low means targeting or message, not volume.
Qualified calls 1 Conversations fine but nothing qualifies means you're reaching the wrong stage of company.
Proposals out 1 per 3 wks Qualified calls that don't become proposals means the offer or the price framing needs work, not the pipeline.
08

This week

Five days. The goal is not a signed client — it's that by Friday the engine is running and the first 60 touches are out.

Monday

Decide the two things that can't be undone later

  • Write down the price — $72K / $96K / $120K — and the rule: discount scope, never rate.
  • Write down the exit trigger: notice on both 1099 engagements the week client two signs.
  • Pick the two verticals in writing and stop hedging on the other two.
  • Buy LinkedIn Sales Navigator. It's the one tool that makes the weekly search a 20-minute job.
Tuesday

Build the proof

  • Write three case studies from your operating history — quota carried, pipeline built, ramp achieved, team scaled. Anonymize logos if you must, never the numbers.
  • Ask both current 1099 clients for documented metrics and a written quote. Do this now, well before you give notice.
  • Rewrite the LinkedIn headline and About section on the wedge: "I'm hired to make myself unnecessary in six months."
Wednesday

Build the lists

  • 250-name network list, tiered T1/T2/T3, with a specific reason-to-reach-out on every T1.
  • 40-name VC and PE platform partner list — named humans with titles, not firms.
  • First 40 target companies from the list in section five plus your first signal-1 search.
  • Set up your own CRM. You are client zero and it should be the demo.
Thursday

Build the machine

  • One-page offer document — three tiers, deliverable set, sendable same-day after a call.
  • MSA and SOW template: 40/30/30, terminal trigger with a date ceiling, IP transfer clause, placement fee disclosed.
  • Load the five message templates into your sequencer with the four-touch, 18-day cadence.
  • Create profiles on Vendux, Go Fractional, Bolster, Continuum, Fractional Jobs. One sitting, then forget them.
Friday

Send

  • 40 network reactivations out — individual, specific, no broadcast post.
  • 10 VC platform partner outreaches out.
  • First 15 signal-qualified cold targets entered into sequence.
  • Book two calls with T1 contacts for next week. Phone, not email.
  • Join Pavilion. Put the first vertical event on the calendar.
What good looks like by Friday

65 touches out, two calls booked, the engine built. Not a client — a client this week would be luck, and the plan doesn't need luck until month three. What it needs is that the Monday block exists, the lists exist, and the templates are loaded, so that week two costs you three hours instead of three days.
