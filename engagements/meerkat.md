## What she sent (verbatim)

In taking a look at Meerkat’s current client list, web site, slide deck and all other relevant materials, I think I understand the hesitation to lean into Healthcare. It’s not perceived as an industry that is fast-moving, agile or has “teeth” from an advertising perspective as so much of Meerkat’s own branding leans into. I’ve seen this happen before, there’s an assumption that “Healthcare” means unsexy in branding. There are so many interesting and innovative healthcare companies that they would align beautifully with.

For starters, being in MN is a major advantage. They’re in Medical Alley and have a dense MedTech cluster in their backyard. There are so many local companies that deeply-align with their agility.

## Voice notes — what her version does that my drafts didn't

- **Audience is "they," not "we."** She writes to Meerkat as an outside advisor about Meerkat, not
  as an insider cheerleader.
- **Opens with diligence and empathy** ("In taking a look at… I think I understand the hesitation")
  before making the case. Validates, then reframes. No rhetorical flourish.
- **Plain, conversational, short.** No punchy closing line, no stacked brand examples in a single
  paragraph — the examples go in a structured list, the prose stays brief.
- **Credibility via parentheticals** ("I have worked with them before," "I know the CEO") — quiet,
  specific, placed next to the target it unlocks. Never a bio paragraph.
- **States her own ranking** ("in order of my perceived importance") — owns the judgment.
- Kept my category structure and one-line descriptors; rewrote all the prose. Next time: give her
  the structured list and a 2–3 sentence plain framing, not a polished persuasive paragraph.
